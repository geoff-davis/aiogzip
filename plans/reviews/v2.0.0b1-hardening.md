## Performance and resource evidence

`plans/benchmarks/v2.0.0b1-candidate.md` compares the retained codec, header,
member, write, scheduler, memory, JSONL, whole-read, and concurrent-read rows
with exact `v2.0.0a4` for stdlib zlib and zlib-ng. All correctness-bearing
digests and sizes match. The initial and reverse-order captures include rows
over 5% and one minimum-statistic result over 10%; all are now enumerated in
the record rather than summarized away. The 10% observation held the evidence
gate closed pending attribution. The same-process A/B/B/A diagnostic retains
all samples, but temporal analysis shows the high-level FCOMMENT row is
non-stationary and cannot adjudicate a 5% minimum threshold. The original and
follow-up raw samples are retained; the release disposition relies on
byte-identical runtime source, not on a claim that a final minimum cleared the
gate.

The apparent 98.52% FCOMMENT peak-allocation increase came from comparing one
sample in the low allocation mode with one in the high mode. Fifteen-sample
follow-ups per source tree and engine reproduced the pre-existing modes on
exact-a4 code and placed baseline/candidate modal medians within 0.06% and
maximum peaks within 0.26%. Scheduler wording was also corrected: the largest
candidate maximum-gap increase is 1.90 us (+0.28%), not sub-microsecond. Peak
memory and scheduler gaps remain within the established gates after these
explicit investigations.

The process-level records also show a +8.08% stdlib minimum-duration change
for the FNAME `tracemalloc` row. That duration is now explicitly enumerated;
the harness defines allocation-row duration as informational and not a
wall-time claim. Its gated peak-allocation metric is covered by the repeated
evidence above.

Review of the first focused diagnostic found that it could mislabel an
unavailable zlib-ng run and did not fully attest source state or preserve
partial results. Clean commit
`4a6fb9f81bfa370c2e8362534f05aadb3630317f` adds an initial active-engine
guard, early exact-commit/describe/dirty checks, atomic checkpoints,
exception-safe codec cleanup, contract-accurate output validation, shared
fixtures, and one-at-a-time header fixture allocation. Its v2 recapture
records exact a4 and that clean candidate commit. Clean commit `daadbd0` then
tightens the engine contract to exact per-field validation and captures the
200-sample v3 original and swapped records. Full-capture minima put FCOMMENT at
-0.83% and canonical +2.00%, but those endpoints do not resolve the row: its
v3 first-half minimum deltas are +13.22% and canonical +8.62%, and quarter
medians expose substantial warm-up drift. FNAME also swings 6.90 percentage
points between the v2 and v3 minimum results. The diagnostic is consequently
inconclusive at 5% for those rows. Canonical full-capture FCOMMENT medians are
more stable (-1.75%, -0.67%, and -1.43%) but remain descriptive.

The `daadbd0` commit extends evidence guards to the shared runner and
comparator. Tooling commit `ab9c977` corrects the request convention:
source-root captures use `--engine`, validate every
default engine field with the macOS CRC exception, reject tracked-dirty source
trees before timing, atomically checkpoint repeat boundaries, and retain the
current row state in a late-failure record. Strict comparisons reject
incomplete, schema-mismatched, dirty, source-inconsistent, or
engine-incompatible captures before reporting timing. `--allow-legacy`
restores access to pre-hardening schema-v2 captures with explicit warnings,
and targeted records can be summarized directly with raw and canonical
minima, medians, first-half minima, and temporal quarters.

An audit of all 129 retained main-harness schema-v2 documents accepts 119 via
`--allow-legacy`, including all four b1 documents in that set. Ten older a2
documents explicitly attest a dirty source tree and remain rejected; legacy
mode does not waive contradictory provenance.

The issue #86 10-byte diagnostic is 0.75% faster with forced stdlib and 0.02%
slower with zlib-ng in the controlled b1 comparison. Same-call write
visibility, failure attribution, cancellation, and poisoning semantics remain
unchanged. `plans/reviews/issue-86-b1-closeout.md` is ready for maintainer use;
no remote issue mutation was performed.

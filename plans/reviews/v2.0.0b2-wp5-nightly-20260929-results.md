Both 64-byte compression rows have three usable, direction-consistent blocks,
with gains far larger than their within-version drift. This addresses the specific
small-item compression regression found in the previous candidate. It does not
close the overall throughput gate or establish a direct paired gain versus f73ae8d.

Random decompression medians range from +1.40% to +2.92%, and 64-byte text
decompression is +1.50%. Record this as residual cost needing disposition, not
zero overhead. The 4 KiB random-decompression row is still inconclusive because
only two blocks satisfy drift. The larger text-decompression changes are small
relative to drift and do not establish a meaningful slowdown merely because their
signs agree. No row crosses the 5% slowdown investigation threshold.

Four rows have only two usable blocks: text compression at 4 KiB, 64 KiB and
1 MiB, and random decompression at 4 KiB. A fifth, random compression at 1 MiB,
has three usable blocks but mixed signs. None is silently accepted by this review.
Do not subtract September 28 and 29 ratios to estimate the new driver's isolated
cost; they came from different series on different nights.

## Interference and host context

At 02:15:06 the runner measured 1.354 foreign CPU cores and rejected WP4 block 2.
At 04:02:30 it measured 1.612 cores and aborted the WP5 warmup. Both exceed the
unchanged 1.0-core ceiling. Earlier preflight spikes delayed starts as designed.

Read-only journal inspection shows ZFS snapshot pruning overlapping the 02:15
sample and Syncoid backup activity overlapping the 04:02 sample. Sanoid is scheduled
every five minutes; Syncoid hourly with up to five minutes randomized delay.
Selected service journal lines and timer definitions are preserved in
`host-context.json` with the archived results. This is timing correlation, not
per-process proof: the runner samples aggregate foreign CPU and cannot attribute
the measured cores to those services. Do not blame storypilot without evidence.

Starting after the hourly backup may reduce one collision risk, but is not a
proven quiet interval. A roughly 12–16-minute series plus 120-second quiet preflight
will not reliably fit between minute 07 and minute 15, and snapshot work recurs.
No backups, snapshot jobs, monitoring thresholds or unrelated processes were altered.

interference decisions and identified the overlapping system jobs. Codex checked
the journal correlation and retained the additional mixed-sign row and scheduling
limitations above. The small-compression finding is addressed for this stdlib
comparison; residual decompression cost and the five inconclusive rows stay open.

The WP4 series has no unstarted slots, so it has no eligible continuation. Never
replay its rejected block. The protocol separately permits one independent
four-block follow-up for inconclusive rows, with the selected case list frozen
before execution and both series retained. This allowance is not an instruction
to schedule it, replace the initial result or keep retrying until a preferred
result appears. A maintainer disposition is another option for unresolved rows.

The WP5 attempt never started a measurement series. Its next attempt would be a
new initial window after review, not a continuation. That comparison is the most
useful next timing work: it isolates the first-step driver's incremental cost
from the previous fairness implementation. Keep the full matrix and current

of deferral. Obtain a new window before registration. Then review before further

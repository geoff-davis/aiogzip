# WP5 timing diagnostic, September 27

The warmed follow-up calibration deferred after its 15-minute start window,
without producing a capture. Its unchanged load threshold prevented completing
120 quiet seconds. This is a deferral, not another failed stability measurement.
The previous 22/32 stability result remains recorded separately.

The maintainer then authorized continuing with a CPU-placement diagnostic after
Claude's methodological review. This experiment identifies useful measurement
controls; it does not qualify a quiet policy or close a release gate.

## Method and provenance

The [archived evidence](data/b2/wp5-placement-20260927/sha256.json) includes hashes
of the exact scratch controller, 12 captures, host telemetry, failed startup record,
and the deferred batched calibration's controller and status. Original absolute
paths in the status records refer to the scratch directories; corresponding capture
basenames are archived alongside them. Source is clean 16af1dc, measurement harness
is clean 5b55e17, and the copied overnight Python 3.14.2 environment is reused.
Each capture verifies its import location and records all three stdlib engines.
The controller is exploratory, not a committed qualification runner.

CPU order was `[0, 13, 13, 0]` repeated three times. Each fresh process decoded
4 MiB of deterministic random data with input items of 1 MiB, 4 KiB and 64 bytes,
in that order. Each case retained eight consecutive batches, each containing at
least three operations and 100 ms of timed work. Complete-output validation remains
outside the timed intervals. No quiet-start gate or system power change applied.
Per-batch CPU counters, process CPU time, frequency snapshots and temperature were
recorded, with an additional host sampler every 250 ms. An initial JSON serialization
failure was corrected before collecting the 12 successful captures; its status and
log are preserved. No successful capture was removed.

## Exploratory findings

For this analysis, exclude the first two batches from each case and compare the
minimum of the remaining six. That analysis choice was made after capture; it is
not a predeclared acceptance rule. All raw batches remain available.

| Item size | CPU 0 range of six capture minima | CPU 13 range |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 MiB | 2.06% | 1.59% |
| 4 KiB | 3.70% | 1.38% |
| 64 B | 0.81% | 1.23% |

CPU 0's 64-byte minima were 219.9–221.7 ms, versus 224.7–227.4 ms on CPU 13.
Neither placement wins across all cases. CPU 0's sibling had more measured activity,
but placement and foreign workload were not independently controlled. Claude
recomputed the spreads and noted that the first capture accounts for much of the
aggregate foreign-load difference and CPU 0's 4 KiB spread. Removing it would be
an exploratory sensitivity check, not grounds for qualifying the run.

Claude also found that frequency snapshots were consistent with the 64-byte speed
difference and with some drift across captures. These snapshots are not effective
clock measurements. Temperature, clocks, other work and capture order covary;
we cannot establish which caused the original calibration's drift or rule out
contention. Fixed case order further confounds case identity with process warmup.
Only stdlib random-data decompression was sampled. No result transfers directly
to compression, text reads, zlib-ng or first-result latency.

## Disposition

Stop repeating the all-cases absolute-stability calibration. Prepare per-gate,
interleaved baseline/candidate measurements with declared warmup, retained raw
samples and explicit inconclusive outcomes. The old experiment remains unsuccessful
under its original criterion; this is a prospective protocol change, not a regrade.
The [qualification design](../reviews/v2.0.0b2-wp5-timing-protocol.md) separates
throughput from latency and preserves the quiet-machine requirement. No new job
is scheduled and no runner policy is changed by this record.

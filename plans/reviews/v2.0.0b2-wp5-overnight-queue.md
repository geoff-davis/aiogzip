## Scheduling and failure behavior

Use the existing dedicated `aiogzip-wp5-nightly-20260927.service` cgroup so the
frozen runner continues accounting for all its children correctly. Replace only
its effective ExecStart via one runtime drop-in after committing the queue runner;
leave the waiting 02:00 timer intact. Preserve KillMode=control-group,
RuntimeMaxSec=4h10m and TimeoutStopSec=15s. No benchmark starts during setup.

Each phase requires 120 seconds with load1/load5 at most 0.5 and foreign CPU at
most 0.25 cores. Busy checks defer ten minutes; interference rejects and retries
that phase's entire attempt. Quiet checks restart between phases, including any
necessary wait for load from the preceding phase to decay. Later failures cannot
invalidate a completed earlier phase. A failed phase may be followed by the next
independent phase if time remains.

All phases share 06:00 as their benchmark cutoff. The frozen runner enforces it;
the queue allows 15 seconds for cleanup and final provenance/status writes before
its independent watchdog terminates an active runner and waits for cleanup. Its summary labels that
phase deferred and retains the runner's own status separately. Unstarted phases
get deferred status files without launching work. If a runner will not settle,
the queue aborts rather than launching another phase; systemd clears remaining
service descendants. No unrelated process is stopped.

The frozen runner limits individual commands to 30 minutes. Prior G07 resource
log creation-to-last-write spans were approximately 27.25 seconds for stdlib and
21.89 seconds for zlib-ng. These are rough setup-planning observations, not latency
qualification. Seven repeats leave substantial expected headroom under that cap;
there is no guarantee that all phases fit after busy-machine deferrals.

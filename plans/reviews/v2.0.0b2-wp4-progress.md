# WP4 pending-line completion

G05 is closed locally following [maintainer qualification](#maintainer-qualification).
Current production candidate and harness: `1645a10`;
baseline: reviewed WP3 `140e661`. These changes have not been pushed.
The initial `68dc67c` candidate and `c9c9ec8` measurements below are historical;
see the large-chunk correction for the current implementation.

## Review correction

The first uncommitted candidate removed bulk transfer for all positive hints.
The maintainer measured a reproducible approximately 25% slowdown in default
`iter_batches()` and correctly rejected closing G05. Their benchmark results
are review evidence, not measurements made by this record's author. The earlier
2,786-pass report preceded the 70 hint cases; the complete suite then contained
2,856 passing cases, one skip, and two expected failures.

The replacement keeps bulk transfer when the remaining hint covers the unread
text-buffer length. Pending lines are a prefix of that unread buffer, so this
is a conservative upper bound computable without visiting any lines. Unlike a
stored batch end, it remains valid when `_append_buffer()` compacts and rebases
the buffer. Buffer replacement drops pending lines, and partial `read()` and
bounded `readline()` invalidate pending state before consuming buffer text.

Every line inspected or copied by the bulk branch is consumed in that call.
The bounded branch likewise visits only consumed lines. Consequently aggregate
line inspections and element copies remain O(N+B), including varying hints and
long lines. No character-based relaxation of the plan's bound is needed, and
no new persistent counter or state restoration obligation is introduced.

## Coverage

The original F4 test is now an ordinary assertion. New instrumentation charges
length inspections, indexed visits, sliced elements, and returned elements,
with a declared bound of six units per line plus two per public call. Alternating
long and one-character lines exercise hints from 1 through 1 MiB and unlimited.
Against baseline source, four of those cases and the original F4 assertion fail;
the other 78 hint/interleaving cases pass.

Public tests cover translated mixed CR/CRLF/LF input, UTF-8/UTF-16, all newline
modes (the generic modes are compatibility controls), whole-line boundaries,
interleaved read/readline/iteration, tell/seek replay, positive-hint transient
rollback, and poisoned-reader complete-line salvage with a partial tail.

The concurrent JSONL integration module now has a 60-second thread watchdog
via the test extra's pytest-timeout dependency. This bounds loops that cannot
receive asyncio cancellation and executor-shutdown hangs. It terminates the
pytest process on expiry, rather than attempting unsafe continuation.
Two identified stale ingest-test processes from the reported deleted scratch
checkout were terminated; unrelated processes were left alone.

## Large-chunk review correction

The maintainer found an 18–28% batching regression at a 4 MiB chunk size in
`68dc67c`. The earlier runner used only the default 256 KiB chunk size and
therefore did not cover that case. Their pinned A/B/C/C/B/A measurements and
prototype results are attributed review evidence, not runs made by this
record's author. Their host also had unrelated storypilot jobs; those results
are not cumulative G17 evidence.

`1645a10` caps the unread-buffer bound at `_LINE_BATCH_CHARS`. Refill uses
`rfind` within that window, so all resulting pending lines together fit in the
window. The fallback for a line longer than the window creates one line and
consumes it immediately, leaving no pending remainder. Therefore the smaller
of unread-buffer length and window size is still an upper bound. Each bulk
inspection still consumes its line in the same call; O(N+B) is preserved
without extra state. A large decoded buffer no longer disables the bulk path.

The synthetic work fixture now fits in the production window. Its four bounded
hint assertions and the original F4 test still fail against WP3 source (five
failures, 96 passes). Three new tests, one for each fast newline mode, require
bulk transfer of the first pending batch while the decoded buffer exceeds the
1 MiB hint. Those three assertions fail against `68dc67c` (three failures,
97 passes). Six refill tests check the pending-prefix/window invariant and
explicitly exercise the singleton over-long-line fallback.

The runner now accepts `--chunk-size` and `--payload varied|repetitive`, records
both in every row, and records inherited CPU affinity. The new matrix covers
default/varied, 4 MiB/varied, and 4 MiB/repetitive JSONL on both engines, including
default iteration, 1 MiB hints, 8 KiB hints, and unlimited reads.

Both full local suites passed: **2,888 passed, 1 skipped, 2 xfailed**. The
strengthened refill test also passed in the focused 100-case module. All-file
prek passed before the implementation commit. No new hosted CI, dependency-floor,
or cumulative G17 qualification is claimed.

## Maintainer qualification

The maintainer reviewed `1645a10` and the evidence in `196ae3e` with no findings,
and authorized closing G05. Their invariant-instrumented full suites passed
with 2,888 passing tests on both engines. They confirmed that the window cap
holds and that the 4 MiB batching regression is gone.

The previously unresolved unlimited-read row is not attributable to this change:
the executed unlimited path is unchanged since WP3. The maintainer's pinned
zlib-ng repeat measured -1.2% overall, with pair deltas alternating around zero.
These are attributed maintainer results, not additional runs by this record's
author. They resolve the WP4 attribution question; the earlier captures remain
intact as historical evidence rather than being rewritten or discarded.

G05 is now closed in the canonical plan and qualification ledger. WP4 remains
committed locally and unpushed. WP5 remains pending, including the two F7
fairness expected failures. G17 remains open: unrelated storypilot jobs have
loaded the machine during recent review runs, and CPU pinning with low drift
does not replace final qualification on a quiet machine.

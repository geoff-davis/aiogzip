as they are reviewed; it does not by itself satisfy the final independent-review

## Settled decisions through the read/write hardening review

### Live `mtime` compatibility correction

The a3 release decision knowingly accepts the `mtime` behavior specified by D5
and D6: the file property follows the most recently completed valid member
header, so concatenated-stream read-ahead can change it beyond the member
containing the bytes returned by a call. This matches the standard-library gzip
reader and is a deliberate compatibility correction despite the normal
alpha-series rule that established asyncio file APIs retain their contract. The
behavior remains documented in the changelog and locked by binary, text,
rewind, corruption, and stdlib-differential tests.

### Reader failure and recovery

All terminal decoder failures are poisoned through one file-layer path whether
they arise during `feed()` or `finish()`. Only format and integrity failures may
salvage already-buffered decoded bytes; decompression limits, cancellation, and
unexpected internal errors fail immediately. `seek(0, SEEK_SET)` on a rewindable
source is the explicit recovery operation and constructs a fresh decoder. Other
seeks on a poisoned reader fail with the close/reopen guidance.

Salvage is explicitly recovery of bytes decoded before the failure, not proof
that their member passed CRC/ISIZE validation. `read(-1)` keeps its buffer and
position transactional across a later failure without retaining a duplicate
buffer for the whole read. Terminal non-validation failures do not recopy bytes
that no read surface may expose. `readline()` accumulates a candidate line in
the shared buffer and publishes bytes and position together only after a
newline, explicit limit, or clean EOF; `readline()`, `readlines()`, and async
iteration therefore retain an unterminated pre-failure remainder and its error.
Recovery guidance mentions `seek(0)` only while the underlying source or
retained compressed cache is rewindable.

Overlapping read or seek calls on one handle are rejected before decoder
reservation without poisoning the stream or discarding another task's live
operation. The reservation remains active across source I/O, codec work, and
result publication. `close()` applies the same rejection while a read is live.
All file-layer overlap rejections use the public `ConcurrentOperationError`,
an `OSError` subtype that callers can catch explicitly before awaiting the
active call and retrying.
Each primitive read surface intentionally inlines only its healthy-path flag
reads; overlap and salvage behavior is centralized in shared cold-path helpers.
Preallocated structural reservation objects pair ownership and release across
every exceptional exit without allocating an async-generator context manager
per call. Primitive binary write shares its codec/sink body through a direct
helper while retaining its inlined reservation; two pinned pairs measured the
changed stress rows at +2.6% and +2.0%, inside the 5% cap. Primitive text write
retains its complete inlined body because the direct helper form repeatedly
exceeded 5% in its individual-write submetric. An unconditional helper call on
the line paths would likewise restore the per-line cost that the prior
`io,micro` review explicitly removed.

### Same-handle write concurrency

One file handle remains single-task-owned. Overlapping `write()`, `flush()`, or
`close()` calls are rejected before codec reservation/finalization and do not
poison the member. The guard remains active through codec completion, sink I/O,
underlying `flush()`, and file-position publication. Composite `writelines()`
and write-mode zero-fill `seek()` retain that same reservation across every
internal batch, so the write/write, write/flush, write-or-flush/close, and
between-batch splice gaps are not left implicit.

Text close checks that binary reservation before finalizing its incremental
encoder and remains open after an overlap rejection, so callers can retry once
the active write completes. Concurrent text closes serialize incremental
encoder and binary-trailer finalization, making close idempotent through its
awaits rather than only before and after them.

A clean binary or text context exit waits for an in-flight call and then owns
normal close/finalization, retrying if a looping producer reserves the handle
again before the waiter resumes. Cancellation in that wait reaches the same
abortive cleanup path before propagating. When an active call instead outlives
a context body that is already propagating an exception, context exit preserves
that primary exception and attempts owned-resource cleanup without discarding
the other task's live codec operation. Every reserved read surface crosses one
shared post-await abort check before any binary or text call can publish bytes,
position, a line, or EOF. Writes and flushes apply the symmetric check before
publishing position or success. An ordinary underlying-close failure preserves
the body exception but leaves both binary and text layers reportably open for
an explicit retry.

The text layer owns an independent preallocated reservation for its incremental
decoder/encoder, newline state, pending-line batch, and decoded-text buffer.
Buffered reads therefore reject overlap even when no binary refill is needed.
Fallible text reads keep their decoded prefix shared until the refill succeeds,
and a sized read rebases its replay origin only after the complete requested
result is recoverable. Composite binary and text `readlines()` restore every
line accumulated before a failed internal read, so a retry cannot skip bytes or
characters after an overlap, transient source failure, or validation error.
Closing the exposed binary `buffer` first does not make text close skip encoder
finalization.

Binary and text close each serialize their complete finalization. A second
close therefore cannot return while the first is suspended writing encoder
tail bytes or the gzip trailer. Public and hot-path text closed state is updated
by a one-shot binary close observer. The binary layer clears that callback
before invoking it, avoiding both a per-line delegated-property check and a
closed text/binary reference cycle.

The final lifecycle pass explicitly audited the symmetry matrix below. A dash
means the layer delegates the contract to the binary implementation rather
than maintaining an independent guard.

| Surface | Overlap ownership | Clean exit | Exceptional abort | Close completion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Binary reads/seek/readlines | Whole-call read reservation | Retry wait loop | Pre-publication error | Binary lock |
| Text reads/seek/readlines | Text + binary reservations | Retry wait loop | Buffered rollback/no partial publication | Binary lock |
| Binary write/flush/writelines | Whole-call write reservation | Retry wait loop | Pre-publication error | Binary lock |
| Text write/flush/writelines | Text + binary reservations | Retry wait loop | Binary error before success | Text then binary locks |
| Binary close | Rejects active I/O | Owns finalization | Retryable abort cleanup | Serialized |
| Text close | Binary preflight | Owns finalization | Binary cleanup/state | Serialized |

### Integration history

This release branch will be squash-merged. The final squash commit must follow
the `CLAUDE.md` subject/body and attribution-trailer requirements. The older
branch-local commits will not be rewritten individually: benchmark records
attest exact intermediate commit hashes, and rewriting those hashes would make
the release evidence misleading without changing the integrated history.

### Release harness failure gates

Incomplete-header cases accept only `gzip.BadGzipFile` as the expected failure;
an unrelated `OSError` aborts the run. The direct-decoder harness applies that
gate across both `feed()` and `finish()`, so feed-time corruption is recorded
per sample instead of terminating the complete benchmark process.

## Explicit post-a3 deferrals

- Keep the decoder notification generation and file-side observed-generation
  mirror required by D4/D5. Their two reset sites and read-only codec properties
  stay test-locked; collapsing them to a timestamp `None` check is post-a3 work.
- Keep `_feed_snapshot()`'s performance-motivated inline preconditions. A
  parameterized parity test now requires identical exception types and messages
  to public `feed()` across not-started, active, finalized, discarded, and
  strict-size states. Consolidating the implementation is post-a3 work unless a
  correctness issue appears.
- Keep `_Operation.__next__()`'s synchronous-hot-path copy of the ownership and
  lifecycle block used by `_advance_raw()`. Lifecycle parity tests exercise
  both the public iterator and async driver; consolidating the two blocks is
  post-a3 work unless profiling changes the current inlining disposition.
- Freeze the a3 release harness's evidence schema through this release. Its
  verification scripts reuse harness helpers, but convergence with
  `run_benchmarks.py` environment/schema machinery is post-a3 cleanup so the
  captured baseline and candidate records are not mutated late in the release.
- Keep the primitive binary and text read guard preambles inline through a3.
  The state-matrix tests lock their unopened, closed, poisoned, and overlap
  behavior, while a shared unconditional helper on line surfaces previously
  exceeded the release's 5% hot-path threshold. Structural consolidation is
  post-a3 work and must be re-measured rather than applied during evidence
  freeze.
- Keep the primitive text `write()` body parallel to `_write_reserved()`
  through a3. Replacing only the body with a direct helper call (while leaving
  reservation checks inline) repeatedly exceeded 5% in the individual-write
  submetric across two pinned `io,micro` pairs. Consolidation is post-a3 work
  unless a different implementation passes the small-write cap.
- Keep the text layer's local closed cache, close-completion flag, serialization
  lock, and one-shot binary observer through a3. Their combined state matrix is
  now test-locked, and collapsing them would reopen the cross-layer lifecycle
  protocol immediately before independent review. Consolidating that state is
  post-a3 work.
- Keep the five surface-local text restore blocks through a3. They preserve
  different combinations of decoder origin, buffered text, pending lines, and
  publication state, and their cancellation/validation/transient-failure
  matrices are test-locked. A shared restore abstraction is post-a3 cleanup.
- Keep `peek()`'s reservation-local refill loop through a3 rather than routing
  it through `_fill_until()`. Consolidating the two would also adopt that
  helper's compaction behavior on a non-consuming surface and needs dedicated
  memory and latency measurement after the release freeze.
- The final pre-D17 review pass (zero correctness findings) surfaced seven
  further cleanup items, folded here rather than applied to the feature-frozen
  candidate. Two are provably-equivalent hot-path guards and must be
  re-measured with the interleaved min-of-N `io,micro` pair when applied:
  guard `_check_read_call_not_aborted()` behind
  `if self._is_closed or self._read_broken:` at the `_readline_reserved`
  publication point and the end of `_fill_until()`, and guard the per-chunk
  `bf._validation_salvage_exhausted()` call in `_read_sized_reserved` behind
  `bf._read_broken`. Five are cold-path consolidations: delete the redundant
  `_sync_closed_from_binary` call sites (the `_closed_observer` callback
  already keeps the mirror coherent on every close path); replace
  `_anext_buffered_reserved`'s verbatim copy of
  `_readline_buffered_reserved`'s refill loop with a delegating call; convert
  the seven-field positional `_readlines_rollback_state` tuple to a
  `NamedTuple` (four interchangeable int slots type-check under transposition);
  hoist sized `read()`'s duplicated pending-lines reset above the fast-path
  branch; and hoist the closed-while-active abort raise shared by
  `_write_reserved`/`_flush_writer` into a cold-path raise-only helper.

These deferrals are intentional release-scope decisions, not unresolved a3
correctness gates. They should be reconsidered after the a3 tag rather than
reopened during final evidence recapture.

## Composite-read and text-state revalidation

The round-ten baseline was clean commit
`9af8a973b492b9393f248583b9c536b52cc86986`; the final candidate was clean
commit `4384571`. The correction remained inside the existing binary/text
wrapper architecture:

- binary and text `readlines()` roll back unpublished complete lines on a
  recoverable failure;
- sized text reads retain the prior buffer until success, then rebase any fresh
  leftover text to its own decoder origin;
- text-buffer compaction accumulates the character replay distance instead of
  pairing a sliced suffix with stale origin metadata;
- text refill helpers consult the binary reader even after its EOF flag is set,
  so poisoned state and remaining salvage cannot be mistaken for clean EOF;
- binary closure synchronizes the text wrapper through a cleared-before-call
  observer, and write-mode zero-fill seek uses the established broken-writer
  error.

The added guard matrix covers binary and text read surfaces after unopened,
failed-open, closed, exposed-buffer-closed, and validation-poisoned states; the
existing parameterized lifecycle matrix covers active-call and context-abort
states. Property tests vary transport/read chunk sizes while requiring exact
text `tell()`/`seek()` replay and stdlib newline classification, including a
file-final bare CR and compaction beyond the 16 KiB threshold.

per-line text paths; the broad iteration benchmark rejected it. Moving the
check into the preallocated refill reservation restored throughput. A later

## Validation-salvage and rejected-call revalidation

The round-eleven baseline was clean commit
`b787fcc9da19665747fa1fea3ae74832b89630ea`; the candidate was clean commit
`78694760857f24a5270a6b03a2ebdc0d68959ae7`. The correction keeps each

- binary `readlines()` rejects overlap before capturing rollback state, and
  its rollback handler executes only while that call owns the reservation;
- decoder output emitted before a later CRC/ISIZE rejection in the same lazy
  `feed()` or `finish()` operation is retained in stream order, including the
  default-chunk small-file case;
- named binary predicates now define validation salvage, failed-call restore,
  and salvage exhaustion for both wrapper layers;
- the binary reader synchronously marks its text owner when poisoning occurs.
  Validation poison preserves decoded recovery text, while cancellation,
  limits, and unexpected terminal errors clear text that no later call may
  publish as healthy data;
- text sized reads stop after draining the binary validation span, producing
  the documented error, data, terminal-error sequence without an intervening
  broken-stream error; and

rejects a competing `readlines()`, and asserts that buffer identity, offset,
and position remain owned by the winner before verifying exact final output.

default-size chunk and require `BadGzipFile -> complete decoded span ->

line-integrity, rewind, text-cookie, and lifecycle matrices remain green.

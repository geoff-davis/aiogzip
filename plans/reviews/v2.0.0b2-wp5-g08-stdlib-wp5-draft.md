## Purpose and status

The [September 29 review](v2.0.0b2-wp5-nightly-20260929-results.md) found no paired
measurement of committed first-step candidate 2f78925 against unchanged WP5
f73ae8d. That series deferred when interference ended its first warmup, before any
block started. Within the streaming APIs, the comparison isolates the first-step
change's incremental cost, including the per-step driver check that decompression
now carries. This 16-case `compress_chunks`/`decompress_chunks` matrix does not
measure or qualify file read/write or inspection paths, although they share that
driver check; those need their own evidence.

This is a new initial series, not a continuation. The September 29 WP5 attempt
has `series_started: false`, so it has no blocks to continue, and the protocol
treats a window deferred before any block starts as producing no series. The WP4
series (aceca4b versus 2f78925) is a different comparison; its rejected block 2
is never replayed, and its blocks are never combined with this series.

## Protocol and policy

The draft reuses the reviewed protocol unchanged:

- all 16 cases: text and random fixtures, compression and decompression, and
  64 B, 4 KiB, 64 KiB and 1 MiB items over 4 MiB payloads;
- two full warmup captures, one per side, then ABBA/BAAB/ABBA/BAAB blocks;
- per case, a 0.25-second/three-operation warmup and nine 0.1-second/
  three-operation batches, discarding the first two and retaining seven;
- stdlib engine, pinned to CPU 13;
- foreign CPU at most one core, load1/load5 at most 0.5, 120 quiet seconds and
  five-second monitoring;
- 30-minute command timeout, block reserve and span, and initial reserve.

Apart from its null window, service name and output path, the draft is identical
to the reviewed September 29 WP5 manifest, including every command. No
production code, runner, comparator, threshold or frozen evidence changed.

## Host timing risk

On September 29, interference coincided with host maintenance jobs, as recorded in
the results review: ZFS snapshot pruning around 02:15 and a Syncoid backup at
04:02. Sanoid runs every five minutes with 15-minute frequent snapshots; Syncoid
runs hourly with up to five minutes' randomized delay. The correlation is timing
only; aggregate foreign-CPU sampling cannot attribute cores to a process.

No start time is a proven quiet interval. A 12–16-minute series plus a 120-second
quiet preflight cannot reliably fit between the hourly backup and the next
quarter-hour snapshot, and snapshot work recurs through every hour. Starting after
an hourly backup avoids only that one overlap. The window may defer or reject
blocks again. The protocol forbids altering unrelated jobs to make a run fit.

# 2.0.2 benchmark table refresh

Status: recorded 2026-11-16. Re-measures the table in `docs/performance.md`
for issue #206 and supersedes the caveat asked for in #161.

## Why

The published table came from the 2.0.0a1 candidate (2026-07-22, Python
3.12.12). The decoder, header parser, read health, text replay origin and the
2.0.2 fairness checkpoints all landed after it, so the table no longer
described released code.

## What was run

- **Sources:** `v2.0.2` (50c8d59), `v2.0.0a1` (9200046) and `v1.11.0`
  (3f23ead), each a clean detached worktree passed as `--source-root`.
- **Runner:** `benchmarks/run_benchmarks.py` at `main` c692654, one venv and
  interpreter for every source.
- **Table captures:** `--category io,scenarios,concurrency --size 8 --repeat 5`,
  for each source on `--engine stdlib` and `--engine zlib-ng`, in three
  rounds with the source order rotated: 18 captures.
- **Full suite:** `--all --size 8 --repeat 3` for `v2.0.2` on each engine: two
  captures.
- **Gate:** before each capture the script waited until the whole machine was
  using less than 0.5 of a core over five seconds. `run.log` has every wait
  and the load average at each start and finish.

All 20 captures completed, with `target_dirty` false in each. They ran between
19:09 and 19:36 PST. The data, the log and `analyze.py` are in
[data/2.0.2-table](data/2.0.2-table/).

## How the figures are derived

These are best-observed comparisons, not paired or typical ones.

- **What a capture reports.** The runner repeats each category and, for each
  benchmark, keeps the metrics of the repeat whose duration was the median.
  That duration is the aiogzip side for the comparative cases, so the `gzip`
  time reported beside it comes from the same repeat and is not `gzip`'s own
  median. The isolated `read(-1)` case also takes the minimum of five reads
  inside each repeat.
- **Ratios against `gzip`.** The lowest reported aiogzip time and the lowest
  reported `gzip` time across the three rounds. The two can come from
  different rounds.
- **Comparisons between aiogzip versions.** The lowest `duration_min` across
  the three rounds, which is the fastest single repeat.
- **Figures from the full-suite captures.** The line-reading times are
  `duration_min` of that one capture. The `fast_compress` throughputs are the
  metrics of its median-duration repeat.

The `gzip` side is the same code in every capture, so its spread across rounds
shows the noise: for example 77.1 to 81.5 ms on the JSONL parse cases.

## Findings

### Against 2.0.0a1

- **2.0.2 holds the a1 numbers.** Of the 28 comparisons above, 24 are within
  4% of a1, bulk text reads are 8% (stdlib) and 13% (zlib-ng) faster, and
  two appear more than 5% slower.
- **Two cases are more than 5% slower, both on zlib-ng and both about one
  millisecond long:**
  - Read-all isolated, compressible: 1.07 to 1.14 ms (+6.9%). The three
    rounds agree (1.1, 1.2, 1.2 ms against 1.1, 1.1, 1.1 ms).
  - Text large reads: 1.44 to 1.57 ms (+8.7%). The 2.0.2 rounds were 2.9, 1.6
    and 2.9 ms, so this case is bimodal and the figure is weak.
- **The micro-tests below confirm the first and not the second.**

### Against 1.11.0

These differences were already present in 2.0.0a1 and are not new in 2.0.2.

- **10-byte binary writes take twice as long.** The
  [a1 candidate record](v2.0.0a1-candidate.md) describes this case as
  deliberately pathological: each call pays the codec's operation-ownership
  accounting, and it is kept as a diagnostic, not a release gate.
- **Line iteration is 4% to 8% slower**, and 100 flushes take 15% to 19%
  longer (about 0.6 ms in total).
- **Highly compressible `read(-1)` on zlib-ng is 45% faster.**

### Against the published table

| Workload | Engine | Published (a1, Python 3.12) | a1 today | 2.0.2 today |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Bulk LF-only text read | stdlib | 1.25x slower | 1.14x slower | 1.11x slower |
| Bulk LF-only text read | zlib-ng | 1.90x faster | 1.69x faster | 2.0x faster |
| Tuned JSONL line iteration | stdlib | 1.79x slower | 1.66x slower | 1.62x slower |
| Tuned JSONL line iteration | zlib-ng | 1.53x slower | 1.42x slower | 1.44x slower |
| Highly compressible `read(-1)` | zlib-ng | 14.5x faster | 12.8x faster | 12.2x faster |

The same a1 source gives different ratios today than when it was published, so
much of the movement in the table does not come from library changes. That is
consistent with the differences in environment and method (Python 3.14.2
against 3.12.12, the zlib-ng package, the machine state, and best-of-three
against a median of five), but this record does not separate them.

The weakest published figure, tuned JSONL line iteration on the stdlib engine,
is now about 1.6x slower than `gzip` rather than 1.8x. It is still the
library's slowest comparison.

## Other figures refreshed

From the three table rounds: batched JSONL parsing was 13% faster than direct
iteration on both engines (75.55 against 86.76 ms on stdlib, 74.73 against
85.70 ms on zlib-ng).

From the `v2.0.2` full-suite captures, one capture per engine:

- `readlines()` over 100,000 lines took 5.2 ms against 16.5 ms for a
  `readline()` loop on the stdlib engine (4.7 against 16.0 ms on zlib-ng),
  each the fastest repeat.
- `fast_compress=True` on text: 54.7 against 43.0 MB/s, 1.27 times faster,
  with output 100.3% of the stdlib size, from the median-duration repeat.

The stdlib full-suite capture shows the batched JSONL case at 1.19x slower
than `gzip`. The three table rounds, with more repeats, all show 0.97 to 0.99,
so that single figure is noise and was not used.

## Micro-tests of the two flagged cases

Run after the captures, the same evening (19:52 to 20:18 PST), with
`data/2.0.2-table/micro/micro.py`: the two cases' fixtures and read calls
from `benchmarks/bench_io.py`, 300 timed iterations after a warm-up, pinned to
one CPU with `taskset -c 8`, in six rounds with the source order rotated.
Times are milliseconds; "best" is the lowest figure over the six rounds.

| Case | Engine | Version | Best minimum | Best median | Range of the six minimums |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Compressible `read(-1)` | zlib-ng | 1.11.0 | 1.480 | 2.059 | 1.480 to 1.524 |
| Compressible `read(-1)` | zlib-ng | 2.0.0a1 | 0.915 | 0.934 | 0.915 to 0.934 |
| Compressible `read(-1)` | zlib-ng | 2.0.2 | 1.013 | 1.029 | 1.013 to 1.029 |
| Compressible `read(-1)` | stdlib | 2.0.0a1 | 18.798 | 19.044 | 18.798 to 19.072 |
| Compressible `read(-1)` | stdlib | 2.0.2 | 18.975 | 19.161 | 18.975 to 19.176 |
| Text sized read | zlib-ng | 2.0.0a1 | 1.781 | 2.032 | 1.781 to 1.819 |
| Text sized read | zlib-ng | 2.0.2 | 1.776 | 2.035 | 1.776 to 1.796 |
| Text long line | zlib-ng | 2.0.0a1 | 1.827 | 2.089 | 1.827 to 1.868 |
| Text long line | zlib-ng | 2.0.2 | 1.837 | 2.099 | 1.837 to 1.872 |

- **Compressible `read(-1)` on zlib-ng is confirmed slower: about 10%, or
  0.1 ms on an 8 MiB read.** The six 2.0.2 minimums (1.013 to 1.029) and the
  six a1 minimums (0.915 to 0.934) do not overlap. On the stdlib engine the
  difference is under 1%. 2.0.2 is still 32% faster than 1.11.0 here.
- **Text large reads are not slower.** Both parts are within 1% of a1 on
  zlib-ng, so the suite's +8.7% was noise from a bimodal case.
- **On the stdlib engine the text cases do not give a clear answer.** The
  2.0.0a1 rounds are stable (minimums 15.2 to 15.5 ms), while the 1.11.0 and
  2.0.2 rounds vary between about 14.5 and 20.5 ms. The best 2.0.2 minimums
  are below a1's, so there is no sign of a slowdown, but the spread is too
  wide to say more.

The cause of the `read(-1)` difference was not investigated. The absolute
times here are not comparable with the suite's: the process is pinned, the
fixture is on a different temporary path, and the suite's timer includes the
`result == payload` check, which the micro-test does after stopping the clock.
The fixtures, sizes, chunk sizes and read calls are the same.

The micro-test JSON files record the imported package path, its version and
the active engines. They do not carry the commit, interpreter and affinity
attestations that the suite captures do; those are as described above and in
`followup.log`.

## Full suite on the older versions

`--all --size 8 --repeat 3` was run on `v2.0.0a1` and `v1.11.0` on each
engine (19:49 to 19:52 PST). Each completed nine of the twelve categories (53
or 54 results) and then stopped in `read_health`, which exercises read
recovery that those versions do not have:

- `v2.0.0a1`: `OSError: gzip decoder is unusable after a prior failure`.
- `v1.11.0`: `gzip.BadGzipFile` from the salvage-read case.

The four JSON files therefore have `status: failed` and hold the nine
completed categories. `text_origin` and `regressions` were not reached.

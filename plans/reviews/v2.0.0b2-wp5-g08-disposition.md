# WP5 G08 disposition

This records G08's evidence and the maintainer's decisions item by item. G08
closed on 2026-10-04 with the
[WP5 completion record](v2.0.0b2-wp5-completion.md#g08-cross-item-fairness).

## Scope

G08 qualifies the BC5 ready-source fairness change on both engines: throughput
against WP4 aceca4b, the first-step change's incremental cost against WP5
f73ae8d, and descriptive scheduling evidence. The candidate is 2f78925. These
series measure the streaming APIs only, not file read/write or inspection paths.

## Settled

- **Starvation repair.** Budgets carried across stream items (`c122df5`) fix
  both original F7 reproductions, and the lifecycle matrix (`e492b4c`) covers
  early exit, source failure, slow destinations and single large items.
- **Small-item compression overhead.** On September 28, f73ae8d was about 5.5%
  (text) and 3.5% (random) slower than aceca4b for 64-byte compression. An
  exploratory split showed the overhead persisting with zero source checkpoints,
  which pointed the investigation at per-item bookkeeping. How the cost divides
  between bookkeeping and the yields themselves remains unresolved. The first-step change
  (`2f78925`) removes the per-item driver setup for compression feeds that
  finish immediately. On September 29, 2f78925 against aceca4b gave −22.97%
  (text) and −14.89% (random) at 64 bytes, with three of three usable blocks
  each ([September 29 results](v2.0.0b2-wp5-nightly-20260929-results.md)).

## Items and decisions

1. **Isolating the first-step change** (stdlib WP5 against 2f78925). Series
   completed and reviewed on September 30
   ([results](v2.0.0b2-wp5-nightly-20260930-results.md)): 64-byte random
   compression -17.75% with four usable blocks, and every decompression block
   within about ±0.9%. The 64-byte and 4 KiB text-compression gains (-27.92%,
   -6.44%) have only two usable blocks each and stay inconclusive; larger
   compression rows show no consistent direction. **Maintainer disposition
   (2026-10-02):** the inconclusive rows are accepted without a speed claim
   ([record](v2.0.0b2-wp5-followup-20260930.md#october-2-outcome-and-maintainer-disposition)).
2. **Residual decompression cost.** Against aceca4b, 2f78925's row medians are
   +1.40% to +2.92% for random decompression and +1.50% for 64-byte text, with
   a consistently positive direction across complete blocks (for example
   +2.38% to +3.81% at 64-byte random, +1.15% to +1.59% at 64-byte text). The
   4 KiB random row stays inconclusive, with two usable blocks. September 30
   found no material first-step decompression regression, which is consistent
   with the residual belonging to the broader WP5 fairness changes but does not
   prove it: the nights are separate series and the changes share driver code.
   **Maintainer decision (2026-09-30): accepted.** The directly observed
   residual of the final candidate against WP4 is accepted without its precise
   cause being proven. The acceptance covers this stdlib residual, not zlib-ng.
3. **Five inconclusive stdlib rows against WP4.** Text compression at 4 KiB,
   64 KiB and 1 MiB and random decompression at 4 KiB have two usable blocks;
   random compression at 1 MiB has mixed signs. **Maintainer disposition
   (2026-10-02):** accepted without a speed claim and without a follow-up
   series; random decompression at 4 KiB falls under item 2's accepted residual
   ([record](v2.0.0b2-wp5-followup-20260930.md#october-2-outcome-and-maintainer-disposition)).
4. **zlib-ng throughput**, against WP4 and against WP5. Batch 1 series
   completed and reviewed on October 3
   ([results](v2.0.0b2-wp5-batch1-results.md)). **Maintainer disposition
   (2026-10-03):** the 64-byte compression gains are claimed; the qualified
   64-byte decompression and 1 MiB compression costs (at most +2.19%) are
   accepted; text-decompression speedups are not claimed, being a negative
   control for measurement noise; inconclusive rows are accepted without a
   speed claim.
5. **Scheduling on both engines**, descriptive, against WP4. Both engines show
   sibling progress where the baseline starved it. **Maintainer disposition
   (2026-10-03):** stdlib's three retained blocks are accepted as sufficient.

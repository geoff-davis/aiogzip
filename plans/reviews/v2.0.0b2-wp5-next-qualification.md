## G06: preserve state before changing string construction

Claude reviewed the proposed fragment accumulator through Herdr, in the existing
aiogzip review tab. Codex received the response directly rather than requiring
the maintainer to relay it. The review called out CRLF across empty decoded
pieces, per-piece replay origins, stateful decoders, failed-read restoration,
readlines rollback, and the differing trailing-CR finalization paths.

It also identified a separate, pre-existing fast-line defect: tell() during an
unpublished read can observe the decoder frontier rather than the line start,
because accumulated fragments are local. Codex independently reproduced this
with a blocked third binary read. The generic modes replay the complete line;
the three fast modes lose the first 200 characters on cookie replay.

`tests/test_wp5_longline_preflight.py` protects the two generic cases and records
the three fast cases as strict `raises=AssertionError` expected failures. Two
additional strict expected failures charge growing-buffer characters for a 1 MiB
generic line, with a linear bound of twice the line length plus one 64 KiB chunk.
`--runxfail` gives exactly five intended assertion failures and two passes.
No desired-error assertion accepts AttributeError, timeout, or setup failure.

The localized repair must preserve observable mid-call cookies as well as
exception restoration. Merely copying the current fast-path accumulator would
spread the cookie defect. The repair and broader encoding/terminator matrix
remain pending; G06 and C0 remain open.

## G07: limits and user guidance

Forty-two new public-API cases cover read, read1, readinto, peek, bounded
readline, text read and text readline, on seekable and nonseekable sources, with
limits below, at and above the complete plaintext size. Two highly compressible
members fit in one source read. Tests check limit rejection, complete round-trip
data, peek non-consumption and the final member mtime. They do not freeze private
buffer sizes as acceptance criteria. All 42 pass locally.

The untrusted-input recipe now explains inflation/read-ahead, early validation
and metadata advancement, per-chunk versus retained-memory bounds, and the need
to limit both decompressed size and concurrent handles. The wider resource,
corruption/salvage and concurrency measurement matrix remains pending under G07.

## G08: overnight evidence scope

`scripts/measure_b2_stream_fairness.py` separates ticker-free throughput from
ticker-enabled scheduling measurements. It includes 64 B, 4 KiB, 64 KiB and 1 MiB
source items, finite empty/tiny sources, empty members and single large items.
Throughput includes both seeded random data and repeated JSONL text; tiny
decompression uses one-byte fragments. A codec-constructor probe records and
checks the selected compression engine module, since public engine_info()
reports the default compression engine rather than the opt-in fast engine.
Every sample verifies full output content outside the timed interval. Scheduling
observations include the final gap even when a baseline starves the ticker.

Use WP4 aceca4b and reviewed WP5 f73ae8d, the same committed harness and copied
environment, both engines, seven samples per capture, in ABBA order. Two baseline
captures permit drift checks. The source/harness must be clean and imports are
checked against the requested source root. Results require review and do not
close G08 or constitute final G17 qualification.

The requested window is September 27, 2026, 02:00–06:00 America/Los_Angeles
(09:00–13:00 UTC). The Linux runner requires a dedicated systemd service,
120 seconds with one/five-minute loads at most 0.5 and foreign CPU at most 0.25
cores, and retries busy starts every ten minutes. It monitors foreign CPU during
each capture, accounting for its own service through cumulative cgroup CPU time.
An interrupted batch is retained and rejected, then retried in full. Only the
runner's own child process group is stopped. If no complete quiet batch fits,
the status is deferred. No unrelated storypilot process is stopped.

The runner's `--check` validates the frozen manifest without executing commands.
The host must stay running; a transient systemd timer survives this chat ending
and user logout (linger is enabled), but not a host reboot. Registration and
result locations are recorded after setup; this document is not an assertion
that the overnight measurements have already run.

Claude's runner review noted that desktop activity alone measured 0.44–0.64 CPU
cores during its daytime sample. The 0.25-core criterion is deliberately retained:
the user asked to defer if busy, and this run may therefore defer even without a
storypilot job. Do not weaken the threshold retrospectively to accept captures.
The review also prompted runner/manifest hash records, fresh-output and
timezone-aware deadline validation, and explicit SIGTERM handling. Final service
configuration uses the user manager and control-group cleanup.

**Compression.** 64-byte random compression has four usable, direction-consistent
blocks with a gain far larger than drift: the first step's intended small-item
gain against unchanged WP5 is supported for that row. 4 KiB random compression is
a small consistent gain. The 64-byte and 4 KiB text gains are consistent in every
complete block but have only two usable blocks, so they stay formally
inconclusive and are not upgraded. Text-compression drift failures are six on
the B side and one on the A side, the largest 3.29%. At 64 KiB and 1 MiB the
compression rows show no consistent direction, which is not demonstrated
equivalence; text block swings there reach about ±2.5%.

**Decompression.** Every decompression block is within about ±0.9%. Most rows
have mixed signs. Two keep one sign in all four blocks, both tiny: random 1 MiB
(median -0.26%) and text 64 KiB (median +0.03%). No row crosses the 5% slowdown
investigation threshold.

## Interpretation and limits

These cases show no material decompression regression from the first-step
change. With the September 29 comparison against WP4, where 2f78925 showed
+1.40% to +2.92% random-decompression and +1.50% 64-byte text-decompression
medians, this is consistent with that residual belonging to the broader WP5
fairness changes, and gives no evidence that the first step explains it.
It does not prove the attribution. The two nights are separate series, not a
matched three-way experiment, and the fairness and first-step changes share
driver code. Do not subtract ratios across nights or claim an exact or zero
contribution from either change.

The maintainer can decide whether to accept the directly observed residual of
the final candidate against WP4 without its precise cause being proven. The
September 29 4 KiB random-decompression row stays inconclusive.

No statistical significance, zero-overhead or G17 claim is made.

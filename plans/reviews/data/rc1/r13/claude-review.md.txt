## 2. Answers

Q1. Native completion before cleanup: yes, with one carve-out BC13
discloses. `_close_underlying` and `_finish_abort` submit the close with
`_submit_native` and await `_settle_before_cancel`, which shields the work,
never cancels it and waits through repeated cancellations. The scan settles
its open (`_acquire_path`), each read (`_read_native`, `_NativeSourceCall`
with `cancel_pending=True`) and its close (`_initial_call`). Probes p1 and p4
confirmed the caller resumes only after the gated native close returned.
Carve-out: a cancellation landing in `_finish_abort` while it is still
settling the active call re-raises before any close is submitted; the handle
stays OPEN and the fd open until garbage collection (BC13: "as before").

Q2. Late I/O results: yes. A scan read completing after cancellation is
retrieved, its bytes dropped, and a worker failure chained as `__cause__`. A
queued read is prevented under the `_NativeSourceCall` lock. After
cancellation no read result reaches the decoder. `track_position=False` only
disables `no_effect` evidence, which the scan never uses. A late path open is
closed exactly once (probe p3).

Q3. Resource ownership and the BC13 latch: yes. The scan owns the source only
when `fileobj is None` or `closefd is True`; a late open is closed inside
`_acquire_path` with no await before the scan's `try`; decoder construction
is inside the protected region. `cancelled_after_close` is set only when
`not work.cancelled() and work.exception() is None` on the executor future
after settlement; a failing close raises before `_mark_closed`, leaving the
handle OPEN. `closefd=False` files are never closed.

Q4. Text seek transaction (BC11): yes. The cursor is `(_position, _decoder)`
with decoder identity kept alive by the tuple. When the binary reader breaks,
binary `seek()` resets `_position`, so the cursor can compare equal, but
`_can_restore_failed_read()` is then False and the poison observer has
already latched and cleared text. The snapshot shares no mutable state.
`_pending_read_origin` is outside the snapshot but excluded by the
`_read_call` reservation. Probes p2 and p5: after a forward seek moves the
cursor and fails, reads, `buffer.read` and text `seek(0, SEEK_END)` raise the
terminal `OSError`; `seek(0)` recovers.

Q5. End-relative seek (BC12): yes. The loop counts unread output, breaks on
EOF, then fills; each byte counted once. EOF still set by the decoder after
trailer validation; `max_decompressed_size` still enforced; a non-HEALTHY
reader rejected before the loop. `BC12_AFTER` matches the shipped loop.

Q6. `writelines()` bound: qualified yes. For exact `str`, `pending` holds only
non-empty strings; `pending_empty` reproduces every write a stored `""` would
have caused on every exit path; bytes identical to b2. Caveat N2.

Q7. Cancellation accounting and reporting (BC15): qualified yes. The uncancel
is in `finally`, guarded by `_source_abort_requested`, consumed exactly once.
Types and precedence unchanged; cleanup failures only as notes. CLI: usage
errors 2, stream errors 1 with `{"ok": false, ...}`, other `Exception` 2. Not
consistent with BC15: the scan's cleanup notes (F1).

Q8. Differential predicates: qualified yes (spot check). BC11 requires an
identical failing seek, both `cursor_moved` witnesses and candidate BROKEN,
and claims only refusals against a reading-on reference until BROKEN ends.
BC12 is metamorphic (`count(BC12_BEFORE) == 1`, prefix agreement) and claims
only `diff(original) − diff(fixed)`. Seed sweeps not re-run; clause S not
audited beyond the ledger.

Q9. Public contract: yes. Manifest byte-identical; no signature, exception
type or dataclass change; `__init__.py` diff is the version only.

Q10. Performance: yes (record review only). R12 discloses the R03 cost, its
attribution, the #128 repair and re-check (+4.46 to +6.52% on `verify()`), and
that `inspect()` was not re-timed after #128. `f7c03c0` has the same
`_inspection.py`, `_source_io.py` and `_opening.py` as `2a350db`; after
`dd70af4` the only `src/` changes are #128 and the version.

Q11. Test-only changes: yes. F-A keeps leak sensitivity; F-B requires strict

Q12. Release inputs: yes. Rebuilt artifacts byte-identical; changelog matches

## 3. Findings

F1 (low, new in RC1): the scan reports a repeated cancellation as a cleanup
failure and loses a real close failure. `_inspection.py:152-164`. A scan
cancelled during a read, then cancelled again during the settled cleanup
close, notes `Source cleanup also failed: CancelledError()` although nothing
failed; if the close raised `OSError`, settlement raises
`cancellation from OSError`, the note still says `CancelledError()` and the
`OSError` is lost. `_close_on_context_exit` (BC15) adds no note for a bare
repeated cancellation and notes its `__cause__` otherwise. Reproduced for
`inspect()` and `verify()` with a gated executor. Diagnostics only. Related b2
defect: `_acquire_path` (`_opening.py:57`) produces
`Opening cleanup also failed: CancelledError()` the same way (probe p3).

F2 (low; mechanism in b2, newly reached by RC1 closes): on Python 3.14+, a
failed settled native close under cancellation also logs "OSError exception
in shielded future". `_settle_before_cancel` (`_codec_async.py:52-75`); from
CPython 3.14 `shield` attaches `_log_on_exception` to the inner future when
the outer is cancelled. Reproduced: 3.12 no handler calls; 3.14
`['OSError exception in shielded future']`, with the correct
`CancelledError` caused by the `OSError` in both. Spurious error-level log
only.

Notes:

- N1: BC11 recovery is broader than "until a seek(0) that completes": a
  positive plain `seek(n)` also leaves BROKEN; `tell()` on a BROKEN text
  handle returns the binary position instead of raising.
- N2: `writelines()` still stores zero-length `str` subclasses
  (`_text.py:2212`), as b2 did; the changelog and code comment overstate the
  bound for subclasses.
- N3: a repeated cancellation while the abort is still settling the active
  call skips the close; BC13 discloses this as "as before".

## 4. Verdict

No blocker, high or medium issues. The two low findings affect diagnostics

to block the RC, but F1 is a cheap fix if the candidate is rebuilt for

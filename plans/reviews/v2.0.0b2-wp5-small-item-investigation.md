# WP5 small-item overhead investigation — September 28

G08 remains open. The first-step compression optimization described below is
retained locally as a candidate. The 256-item fairness ceiling, codec budgets and
offload thresholds are unchanged.
No further overnight window has been scheduled.

## Evidence and limits

The September 28 stdlib throughput window completed four blocks. The 64-byte text
compression median elapsed-time increase was 5.50%; three blocks met the drift
screen and all four ratios were positive. Provenance and host-policy checks passed.
The increase requires investigation, not an automatic gate failure or acceptance.

Two local exploratory diagnostics followed. Their scripts, raw captures and
summaries are preserved in the [evidence archive](../benchmarks/data/b2/wp5-small-item-20260928/README.md),
with working copies under the repository's ignored `.cache` directory:

- `wp5-checkpoint-split-20260928`: six balanced orders of baseline, WP5 and WP5
  with the source-item ceiling disabled in process. Text compression still showed
  a 5.89% median increase against baseline with zero checkpoints, versus 6.23%
  with normal checkpoints. The direct checkpoint-on/off differences varied in
  sign. This supports investigating bookkeeping but does not establish a precise
  yield cost, zero yield cost, or exclusive causal attribution. The random cases
  gained replacement codec checkpoints when source checkpoints were disabled.
- `wp5-lazy-budget-20260928`: four paired orders testing deferred loading and
  writeback of codec-budget fields for empty operations. Median changes against
  WP5 were -0.61% text compression, approximately zero random compression and
  +1.14% random decompression. Benefit was unresolved; the production patch was
  reverted and retained with its hash in the diagnostic directory.

These daytime runs used CPU 13, the copied interpreter/environment, validated
outputs and separate checkpoint instrumentation. They did not screen foreign CPU
and are not qualification evidence. The second used explicitly dirty source;
its production patch was held unchanged during timing and archived afterwards.
Do not replace the overnight 5.50% observation with these diagnostic percentages.

The retained test addition inserts empty operations between operations carrying
codec budget, across all four codec budget thresholds. The focused streaming and
fairness suite passes 149 tests; all-file prek passes. No full-suite claim is made
for this test-only addition yet.

## Claude's independent investigation

Claude reviewed both experiments through Herdr and then independently examined
executed bytecodes and first-step outcomes using non-timing scratch probes.
It reported approximately 55 additional executed instructions per tiny compression
item in WP5 versus WP4, with overhead split between the stream loop and driver.
The rejected lazy patch removed some instructions for compression but added work
for decompression. These are structural observations from Claude's probes, not
independently reproduced timing measurements or instruction-cost estimates.

Its first-step probes found nearly all tiny compression feeds finished without
output or progress. This suggests eliminating the per-feed async-generator
protocol may have more value than eliminating a few slot accesses.

## Ranked next experiments

1. **Small compression feed first-step fast path.** Advance once synchronously
   below the existing compression offload threshold. If the operation is done,
   avoid constructing the async driver. Otherwise hand the first result to the
   normal driver for accounting and delivery. Keep start, finish, decompression
   and file callers on their current paths initially. Claude recommends this
   first; Codex agrees it is the strongest next experiment, not a proven speedup.
2. **Local source counters.** Keep item/byte counts in stream locals, with explicit
   notification when a codec checkpoint resets the shared budget. A reset
   generation is one possible design. This saves some attribute operations but
   adds synchronization bookkeeping; measure it separately if needed. A missed
   reset changes checkpoint cadence and must not be accepted as equivalent.
3. **Persistent stream driver.** Keep driver counters local across multiple feed
   operations. This could help decompression too, but changes substantially more
   ownership and cleanup code. Defer unless the smaller changes are insufficient.

Do not raise the fairness ceiling, remove the byte backstop or revive the lazy
patch on the current evidence.

## Conditions for the first-step experiment

- Preserve the source count and checkpoint-before-next-pull ordering, including
  empty items. Do not introduce read-ahead or a producer task.
- At or above the existing offload threshold, retain executor dispatch. No large
  native step may accidentally execute inline.
- A first-step failure must close the operation before encoder discard, preserve
  the original failure and suppress a simultaneous close failure as today.
- A nonterminal first result must pass through the existing byte/progress
  accounting and checkpoint-before-yield logic exactly once.
- Consumer exit after that result must close the operation; cancellation and
  executor settlement semantics must remain intact.
- Test first-step failure/close failure, threshold offload, early consumer exit,
  unchanged budgets on immediate completion and cross-operation checkpoints.
  Run existing fairness, cancellation and lifecycle coverage.
- Compare all G08 cases, including 64 KiB and 1 MiB controls, against both WP4 and
  unchanged WP5. Confirm checkpoint counts and validated outputs. Any retained
  production candidate requires full checks and clean-source qualification;
  exploratory timing alone cannot close G08.

## Retained first-step candidate

Small compression feeds now advance once inline below 256 KiB. An immediate
completion skips creating the async driver; a nonterminal result enters the
existing driver accounting exactly once. First-step errors close the operation
before encoder discard and preserve the original failure even if close fails.
Start/finish, decompression and file callers do not pre-advance. The shared driver
has one additional first-result branch, including on its existing callers.

Claude reviewed the implementation and the additional tests without finding a
correctness blocker. The tests cover threshold boundaries, ordinary/cancellation
failures with simultaneous close failures, accounting before another advance,
empty-operation budgets, mixed real feeds versus the unprimed path, and consumer
exit. Consumer exit preserves existing behavior: encoder discard invalidates the
active operation before source closure; correctness does not depend on the later
async-driver finalization.

A four-pair BD/DB exploratory comparison covers all 16 G08 throughput cases.
B is clean f73ae8d. D is a frozen dirty worktree at 6f5be82 plus the archived
first-step patch. The intervening text-reader changes are outside these streaming
APIs. The exact initial patch and its hash were captured before measurement.
Every case's source and codec checkpoint counts match in all four pairs.

| Case | Median elapsed-time change | Paired range |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| text compression, 64 B | -26.74% | -27.85% to -26.06% |
| random compression, 64 B | -17.51% | -18.96% to -15.95% |
| text compression, 4 KiB | -7.25% | -8.51% to -6.84% |
| random compression, 4 KiB | -0.73% | -1.31% to +2.74% |
| text decompression, 64 B | +1.09% | -0.50% to +1.39% |
| random decompression, 64 B | +0.40% | -0.44% to +1.63% |

All other row medians are between -1.48% and +0.40%; the full ranges and raw
samples remain in the evidence. Mixed-sign controls do not prove equivalence.
Random 1 MiB decompression happens to improve in all four pairs (-2.56% to
-0.90%) despite having no intended optimization; do not attribute that to this
change. The random 64 KiB compression range includes one -5.35% outlier.

After freezing the timed source, the working candidate was refined to use None
for "no precomputed result", distinct from actual _DONE. A supplied_DONE now
terminates without advancing again, with its own regression test. Claude reviewed
this refinement and the real-feed/consumer-exit additions. The refinement is not
in the timed copy and must be included in later clean-source qualification.

The large, consistent small-compression gains justify retaining the candidate for
qualification. They do not establish its performance against WP4, its zlib-ng
performance, or unchanged file-path performance. G08 and G17 remain open. No new
window, release decision, or push follows from this diagnostic.

Final verification of the retained None-sentinel implementation: both full suites
passed 3,551 tests with one skip each (default and forced-stdlib engines). Claude
independently recomputed all 16 timing rows and verified manifest/patch hashes,
pins and checkpoint-count equality without a blocking finding. Raw artifacts
from the overnight run and all three diagnostics are archived byte-for-byte in
the evidence bundle linked above. No zlib-ng timing or final qualification is
claimed by these correctness runs.

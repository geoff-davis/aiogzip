### Repeated-member inspection

The initial candidate exposed a repeatable 52-55 ms many-member result.
Profiling identified general incremental-header and retained-span setup on
each of 1,001 ordinary members. The final candidate adds a validated
contiguous plain-header path while retaining the incremental path for
fragmented/optional headers, elides unused FHCRC accounting, and reuses one
cleared private parser/span shell.

Immediate nine-repeat a1/candidate pairs measured:

| Engine | Operation | a1 | a2 | Change |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| stdlib | verify | 25.151 ms | 26.752 ms | +6.4% |
| stdlib | inspect | 29.202 ms | 29.641 ms | +1.5% |
| zlib-ng | verify | 23.048 ms | 24.100 ms | +4.6% |
| zlib-ng | inspect | 25.729 ms | 26.792 ms | +4.1% |

The `_read_discard (many-members)` control remained +18.2% versus a1 under
stdlib and +6.1% under zlib-ng. It materializes the complete 8 MiB output and
is a diagnostic control in the inspection suite, not the bounded inspection
or verification API. The representative full-read memory sentinel remains
below its historical ceiling. This residual control delta is retained in the
raw record and is not used to claim that every synthetic many-member timing
is identical.

### Tiny-write diagnostic

The 838,860-call, 10-byte write case remains materially slower than v1.11.0.
Depending on the bracket and governor state, the measured delta ranged from
about +55% to +161%. The primary five-repeat result was +116% with forced
stdlib, consistent with the earlier profile.

The hard guard is against a1, not v1. Immediate nine-repeat comparisons put
the candidate -7.7% versus a1 in the stdlib run and +3.8% in the zlib-ng-active
run, both within the +10% guard. Each accepted write intentionally owns and
completes one deterministic codec operation. Cross-call buffering would
change sink-error timing, so it remains deferred. This is an accepted
non-blocking diagnostic miss, not described as fixed.

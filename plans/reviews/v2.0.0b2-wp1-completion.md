# WP1 completion: native-work settlement

G02 is complete locally. The final repair is `f39fb8e`; the initial safe repair
and shared measurement harness are `c27b48d`. Only `_codec_async.py` changes
production behavior. F2/F3 and the remaining work packages are still open; C0

is claimed.

## Correctness and compatibility

The driver now directly retains a shielded executor future until native completion.
It no longer creates a helper task. Repeated cancellation, cancellation of the
shield waiter, and actual runner shutdown cannot make running or queued native
work appear finished. Cancellation remains the outcome; a simultaneous worker
failure is retrieved and retained as its cause. Cleanup and input release follow
native settlement. See the [ownership design](../design/v2.0.0b2-native-settlement.md)
and [BC1 ledger](v2.0.0b2-behavior-exceptions.md#bc1-selected-correction).

## Paired performance and disposition

Baseline source is `dc8950c`; final candidate is `f39fb8e`. Both were measured from
clean trees with the same clean `c27b48d` harness, interpreter and dependencies.
Each engine ran A/B/B/A: baseline, candidate, candidate-reverse, baseline-reverse.
Each case retains 21 samples, median and MAD, exact fixture hashes and engine
identity. Timing is sequential, with warmup, fixture creation and correctness
validation outside the samples. These are direct codec-driver measurements using
its default offload threshold, not file-reader performance qualification.

The first safe implementation retained the helper and added a shield. Its separate
[raw records](data/b2/wp1/initial-helper/) contain median deltas of +1.6–33.3%,
but shared-host drift prevents attributing that range to the helper. Removing it
eliminates one task per offloaded step by construction; that structural reduction
is the justification for the simpler design. These observations remain archived
and are not counted as final-candidate results.

The four observed step-latency median deltas are negative, but they do not establish
a 4.7–20.2% improvement: identical-baseline step medians drifted -8.8% (stdlib)
and -8.7% (zlib-ng). The stdlib reverse-pair delta falls within that drift. Minimum
times show zlib-ng step deltas of -10.6% and -9.5%, the most consistent indication
of improvement here, but still not quiet-machine qualification. Caller-only
cancellation medians differed by -0.5% to +0.7%; this separate instrumented probe
measures cancellation plus gate release through real encoder work and cleanup,
excluding the gate wait. It does not compare safe settlement with unsafe early
return, or promise a bound for uninterruptible work.

The final captures had one-minute load averages of 4.6–4.8, so they did not meet
[CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md)'s quiet-machine guidance. The unchanged 16 KiB inline
controls vary substantially. At 4 MiB, identical-baseline decode medians moved
-11.3% (stdlib) and -2.2% (zlib-ng) between runs. Stdlib decode's minimum-time
candidate deltas are -38.2% and +6.8%; zlib-ng's are +35.3% and -1.0%. These
sign changes across pairs and statistics make the decode results inconclusive.
The table retains the original medians; minimum-time comparisons are a sensitivity
check, not a replacement statistic selected to favor the candidate.

These observations establish neither a candidate-attributable hard-gate regression
nor a general speedup. G02 remains closed on correctness and the structural
reduction in scheduled work. G17 remains open for quiet-machine cumulative
baseline/C0/final qualification with interleaved runs and minimum-time comparisons;
no performance-policy exception is asserted.

Cancellation can still wait for queued or uninterruptible native work. WP2 must
apply authoritative settlement to source I/O separately; this codec repair does
not resolve consumed-input loss.

## Maintainer review follow-up

The maintainer reported no WP1 correctness findings and confirmed G02 closure.
Their additional runs, attributed to their review rather than execution in this
session, were:

- Full default and forced-stdlib suites: 2,520 passed, 1 skipped, 14 xfailed each.
- Settlement/cancellation coverage on Python 3.11 and 3.12: 404 passed, 6 xfailed each.
- Settlement/native-I/O tests repeated 30 times without failures.

The review also confirmed that cancellation counts preserve timeout and external
cancellation behavior. The historical F1 fixture now issues repeated cancellation
of the same caller task; separate shield-waiter cancellation is covered by the
native settlement matrix.

For WP2, native aiofiles reads can reuse the private-future settlement mechanism,
but must retain the returned bytes after cancellation. Custom async sources can
lose consumed data inside their own cancelled `read()`; that path requires terminal
reader failure rather than a retry that could accept only a later member. These
are implementation constraints for WP2, not fixes supplied by WP1.

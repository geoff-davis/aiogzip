# WP5 provisional one-core quiet policy

Following the reviewed tooling push, the maintainer accepted proceeding with the
recommended provisional one-core ceiling and separate review of result usability.
This is a prospective operational policy for the new WP5 block runner, not a claim
that the earlier calibration succeeded. G06–G09 and G17 remain open.

## Exact change

Raise the maximum foreign CPU consumption from 0.25 to 1.0 cores, averaged over
each at-most-five-second monitoring interval, subtracting the dedicated service's
cgroup CPU consumption. Exactly 1.0 is allowed; greater than 1.0 rejects active
work or resets the quiet period. Preserve load1/load5 at most 0.5 and the 120-second
continuous quiet-start requirement. Load limits apply before work, while foreign
CPU is monitored during work so the benchmark's own load does not reject it.

No additional automatic relaxation is permitted. The runner still requires its
exact declared policy, and the manifest generator copies that policy. Old dated
manifests, the September 27 runner, frozen worktrees and unsuccessful evidence
remain unchanged. Existing drafts generated at f756810 retain the old ceiling;
they must be superseded with newly generated manifests from the new runner commit
before use, not silently rewritten under their old hashes. No date is selected and
no timer or service is registered by this policy change.

## Rationale and limits

All 24 overnight preflight samples exceeded 0.25 cores (about 0.33–0.81), preventing
any measurements. The first one-core calibration completed but failed its original
absolute-repeatability rule: only 22 of 32 rows stayed within 3%. The warmed follow-up
deferred without captures because it could not complete the unchanged load check.
The subsequent placement diagnostic supports controlling warmup and CPU placement,
but does not establish that a one-core ceiling makes a host quiet or explain the
original drift causally. Preserve those outcomes exactly as recorded.

The new ceiling permits a controlled attempt under moderate background activity;
it does not qualify the resulting evidence. Global CPU consumption does not locate
contention on the pinned CPU or its sibling, nor capture all shared power effects.
The block runner retains per-core/sibling counters, frequency snapshots, temperature
where available, load and global accounting. Review those alongside results.
The unchanged load rule can still defer a run; this change does not promise that
any particular window will start or complete.

## Usability remains a separate decision

For G06/G08 throughput use the reviewed four-block protocol, declared warmup/batching,
common CPU placement, within-version drift screens and explicit inconclusive results.
Investigate median slowdowns over 5% even when noisy. Retain rejected blocks and
failed attempts, and honor bounded follow-up rules. Do not treat a successful service
or a `review-required` report as a gate pass. No retrospective threshold changes or
sample selection can turn the failed calibration into qualification.

For G08 scheduling and G07 first-result latency, throughput drift checks are not a
substitute for review. Retain all single-operation/fresh-process observations and
assess distributions, ticker progress, contemporaneous host telemetry and existing
resource/limit evidence. Document interference and variability; results insufficient
to support the required disposition leave the gate open. This provisional ceiling does not waive the original plan's quiet-machine
requirement: if host telemetry or timing variability leaves that requirement
unsubstantiated, keep the observations diagnostic and the gate open pending an
explicit disposition. G17's final release qualification policy is unchanged.

The next scheduling step needs newly pinned manifests, explicit future windows,
service bounds and preflight checks from clean dedicated worktrees.

**S2. **S3. 1.x→2.0 migration guide is incomplete.** `docs/migration.md:46-52` says "Ordinary asyncio callers do not need to change their code… The main compatibility change is the Python 3.11 floor." Related stale text: `docs/index.md:136` still says handle state is "mutated without locking — the same contract as standard-library file objects" (2.0 now rejects overlap first); `examples/README.md:46` names `dist/aiogzip-2.0.0b2*.whl`. *Docs only.*

The b2 plan §17 keeps "public feedback intake" and "external RC review" on the RC path; the rc1 candidate review (lines 226-228) recorded no public reports and that Codex/Claude cross-review stands in for external review. ### Bottom line
After M1–M3, with M4 holding, the project is ready for 2.0.0 and no rc3 is needed: every remaining item is metadata, docs, workflow, or process. Three review rounds, differential sweeps, the hosted matrix, and my local both-engine runs found nothing release-blocking in `src/`, and the known LOWs are safe to ship. The biggest practical risks are release-mechanics rather than code: beta wording frozen into the PyPI page, a docs site cluttered with dev snapshots just as `latest` flips to 2.0, a record-hash mismatch if #146 isn't merged first, and 1.11 users meeting 2.0's stricter concurrency and cancellation behavior without a consolidated migration note. Only a desire to fix the S08 LOWs (or any other `src/` change) before 2.0.0 would justify an rc3, and I recommend shipping those in 2.0.1 instead.
